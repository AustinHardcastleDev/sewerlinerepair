# 03 — Codes, permits, and compliance

Reviewed: 2026-08-12

## Executive summary
Most U.S. jurisdictions adopt a version of the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), often with local amendments. Permits and inspections are local even under statewide codes. Work in a street, sidewalk, easement, or right-of-way may require separate municipal approval. Public utility marking (811) and private lateral locating are different scopes.

## Detailed findings
- Code edition and amendments must be verified per state/city before citing an edition in public copy.
- Lateral ownership ordinances are often municipal, not statewide.
- Contractor licensing may be plumbing contractor, specialty sewer, or excavator depending on state.
- Confined-space and excavation safety obligations apply to contractors (OSHA); do not publish DIY instructions.

## Claims we can make
- Permits and inspection are local even when a state adopts a statewide plumbing code. (IPC/UPC adoption pattern)
- 811 marks public utilities; private lateral locating is a separate contractor responsibility unless the local program says otherwise. (Call 811)

## Claims to avoid
- Nationwide permit price or turnaround time.
- DIY excavation or sewage-handling steps.
