# 02 — Costs and pricing

Reviewed: 2026-08-12

## Executive summary
Published “national average” sewer repair prices are marketing composites, not permits or bids. Cost drivers that can be discussed safely: diagnosis (camera + locate), access (yard, slab, driveway, street), method (lining, bursting, open cut), length/depth/diameter, restoration, permits, and right-of-way work. Do not publish a single national dollar figure as typical.

## Detailed findings
- Diagnosis and locating often precede a firm repair quote.
- Surface restoration (landscaping, concrete, asphalt) can rival or exceed pipe work.
- Street/sidewalk/easement work adds municipal fees and restoration standards.
- Emergency backup response differs from planned lateral replacement pricing.

## Claims we can make
- Cost depends on diagnosis, access, method, pipe length/depth, restoration, permits, and right-of-way factors. (industry practice; municipal fee schedules vary)
- Readers should request itemized scopes separating pipe work from restoration and fees. (buyer guidance)

## Claims to avoid
- Any unsourced national average repair or replacement price.
- Guarantees that trenchless is cheaper than excavation in every case.

## Known gaps
- Local permit fee tables change; capture city-specific fees only with dated primary URLs.

## Primary sources
- Municipal permit fee schedules (city-specific)
- State contractor licensing boards (who may bid)
